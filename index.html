Joshua
Ange

I am a first-year PhD student in the Department of Physics at Columbia University, advised by Colin Hill. My research centers on the cosmic microwave background, large-scale structure, and what the universe's oldest light can tell us about its composition and evolution.

Previously I completed an MPhil at Cambridge (Churchill Scholar) with Boris Bolliet and Blake Sherwin, and a B.S. in Physics and Mathematics at SMU with Joel Meyers.

Research

CMB Cosmology & Large-Scale Structure

I work on extracting cosmological information from CMB temperature and polarization maps, including delensing, higher-order lensing statistics, and thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich cluster counts. A current focus is using tSZ cluster abundances to disentangle ultra-light axion dark matter from baryonic feedback.

CMB Delensing tSZ Dark Matter Baryonic Feedback

CMB-HD Foregrounds

I have contributed to foreground simulations and point-source detection and removal pipelines for the proposed CMB-HD next-generation telescope, which will map the microwave sky at arcminute resolution with unprecedented sensitivity.

CMB-HD Foregrounds Source Removal

Quantum Information & Instrumentation

Earlier work included photonic quantum computing, boson sampling for random number generation on the Xanadu X8 processor, and detector characterization for the SuperCDMS SNOLAB dark matter experiment.

Quantum Photonics Boson Sampling SuperCDMS
